Customer Churn Prediction (MSc Project)

R · Logistic regression · Decision tree · caret

Question: Which telecom customers are likely to cancel, and which factors are linked to it?

Share of churners correctly detected on the test set

  • Split 7,032 customers 80/20 (5,625 for training, 1,407 for testing) to check the models on unseen data.
  • Compared a logistic regression and a decision tree using seven variables: contract type, digital billing, internet service, technical support, online backup, TV and months as a customer.
  • Both models reach about 79% accuracy (79.3% vs 78.6%), only modestly above the 73.4% obtained by always predicting that the customer stays.
  • The logistic regression detects more churners (199 of 374, 53%) than the tree (176 of 374, 47%), while the tree raises slightly fewer false alarms (103 vs 117). The best choice depends on the cost of a missed churner versus an unnecessary retention offer.
  • The strongest drivers are contract type and time as a customer: month-to-month and newer customers are the most likely to leave. Fiber optic customers also churn more than DSL customers, and having technical support is linked to lower churn.

E-commerce Sales Performance Analysis

SQL · Python · Reporting

Question: Which acquisition channels and months drive revenue, and where is value being lost to returns?

SELECT channel,
       COUNT(*)                                   AS orders,
       ROUND(SUM(revenue))                        AS revenue,
       ROUND(AVG(revenue), 1)                     AS aov,
       ROUND(100.0 * SUM(returned) / COUNT(*), 1) AS return_rate_pct
FROM orders
GROUP BY channel
ORDER BY revenue DESC;
  • Organic Search is the largest channel at €350k (32% of €1,107k total revenue).
  • Email has the highest average order value (€74), so it deserves more investment than its order volume suggests.
  • Social has the highest return rate (15.0%), which erodes its margin.
  • November and December generate 28% of annual revenue, so stock and campaign planning should be front-loaded.

Customer Segmentation with RFM Analysis

Python · pandas

Question: Which customers are most valuable, and which are slipping away?

  • Champions are 22% of customers but 49% of revenue.
  • "At risk" customers (previously frequent, now inactive) represent €128k of historic revenue, the best target for a win-back email flow.
  • Recommended actions: early access and loyalty perks for Champions, personalised reactivation offers for At-risk customers, and a second-purchase journey for New customers.

Which Market Has Grown the Most?

Python · Time-series reasoning

Question: Given monthly sales by country, which market has really grown the most? Which time window is fair, and how should a one-off spike at the start be handled?

1. Naive: December vs January revenue

2. Second half vs first half of the year

3. Change in share of monthly revenue, February to December (removes seasonality)

  • January contains a one-off spike in UK orders: the UK is 41% of that month's revenue versus 29% on average for the rest of the year, so January is not a fair starting point.
  • The naive Dec-vs-Jan comparison shows Germany at +249% and the UK at only +19%. Both figures are exaggerated: the UK baseline is inflated by the spike, and December seasonality lifts every market.
  • Conclusion: Germany has grown the most (+5.8 pp of revenue share). Both robust methods agree. I would exclude the January anomaly and control for seasonality before comparing markets.

A/B Test: Localised Spanish Product Pages

Python · Statistical testing

Question: Does serving fully localised Spanish product pages increase conversion for visitors in Spain?

  • Conversion rose from 2.92% to 3.73% (relative lift +28%) across 25,000 visitors per variant.
  • Two-proportion z-test: z = 5.04, p < 0.001; 95% CI for the difference: 0.49 to 1.12 percentage points. The result is statistically significant.
  • At this rate, every 100,000 visitors would bring about 808 extra orders (about €49,677 at an average order value of €61).
